## Premise

A delegation of Orlesian merchant houses and Antivan trade princes arrives at Skyhold to discuss supplying the march on Adamant. Josephine is nervous, Dorian is useful, and Sera is somewhere in the rafters.

## Stakes

The Inquisition needs grain, iron and credit for a long campaign. A good agreement ties the great trading houses to the cause; a bad one makes the Inquisition a client of people who only wish to sell.

## Beats

1. The delegation's arrival in the great hall, with banners, gifts and polite hints about price.
2. A tour of Skyhold in which every merchant is measuring something other than the architecture.
3. Dorian reads the room's old rivalries; Sera has already learned who tips and who does not.
4. A private bargain is offered, generous and slightly poisoned.
5. Dinner in the hall settles the matter, or breaks it.

## Choices

- Favour the Orlesian houses or the Antivan princes: Orlesian houses give steady supply and expect influence; Antivan princes give flexible credit and expect favours.
- Accept the private bargain or refuse it: accepting brings faster wagons for the march and a hidden debt; refusing keeps independence.
- Let Sera's mischief play out or quietly stop it: playing it out may win respect from an unexpected quarter or cause an incident.

## Encounters

none.

## Outcomes

The supply lines for Adamant are secured, or limited, according to the choices. Josephine's war-table project sway-merchant-princes is resolved. The great houses remember how the Inquisition treats trade.

## Low-energy version

Play it as the dinner alone: the Inquisitor at the table with Josephine, Dorian and Sera, a few polite negotiations and one chosen toast.
