## Premise

Cullen's first full drill of the new recruits runs long, and the yard has the look of a place where nothing quite worked and everyone is proud of it anyway. Afterwards, Blackwall sits on a barrel whittling and invites the Inquisitor to join him while the recruits eat.

## Stakes

Nothing is urgent. The point is the pause: recruits learning that tired is not failure, and a commander learning that stopping is part of the work.

## Beats

1. The last drill ends in a heap of laughter and wooden swords. Cullen calls it a decent first day and clearly means it.
2. Blackwall offers the Inquisitor a knife and a block of wood. His carving is a bird; the first attempts are lumps.
3. Recruits drift over with stories, bad ones, and a half-serious wager on how long Cullen will pretend to disapprove of the singing.
4. Cullen sits, finally, and says something dry about sitting.
5. The light drops over the yard. Nobody leaves yet.

## Choices

- Carve something, tell a story or just listen: any of them is welcome.
- Ask Cullen about the yard's early days or let him rest in quiet: either way, he relaxes.
- Join the singing or watch from the wall: both count as belonging.

## Encounters

none.

## Outcomes

The recruits feel seen and Cullen trusts the Inquisitor a little more. Blackwall remembers the Inquisitor sat with him. The garrison's morale quietly rises.

## Low-energy version

This is already the low-energy version. Play it as a slow conversation on the wall or by the barrels: no tests, no combat, just a seat, a snack and three people who are glad to be there.
