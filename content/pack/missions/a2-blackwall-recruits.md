## Premise

Blackwall asks the Inquisitor to come to a Hinterlands crossroads where a dozen farmers and a few ex-soldiers have turned up to join the Inquisition. Most have never held a sword, and he wants help deciding who to send to Cullen and who to send home with a better plan.

## Stakes

Good recruits become the garrison that holds Skyhold. Poor ones get hurt, and these particular volunteers are exactly the people Blackwall cannot stand to fail.

## Beats

1. A muddy field and a nervous line of volunteers; Blackwall gruffly shows how he talks to people who are afraid.
2. One recruit is quietly too old, one too young, and one is clearly hiding a limp.
3. A rough practice, wooden swords and fence rails, shows who has patience and who has temper.
4. Blackwall mentions a warden's duty in passing and then says nothing more. His discomfort with being called an authority is noticeable, and there is a flicker of something older that he deflects.
5. At the end of the day he is quieter than the day warrants, and offers to carry the unlucky ones' packs.

## Choices

- Take every volunteer or choose carefully: taking all swells the ranks and strains the quartermaster; choosing carefully keeps standards and disappoints some.
- Find work for those turned away or leave it to the farmers: work builds goodwill in the Hinterlands, and the farmers send word of Venatori movements.
- Ask Blackwall about himself or let the silence be: asking earns a guarded answer; letting it be earns his gratitude.

## Encounters

none.

## Outcomes

A new company of recruits marches for Skyhold, and Cullen notes their arrival. Blackwall trusts the Inquisitor a little more. Nothing about his past is confirmed.

## Low-energy version

Play it as a quiet evening on the crossroads: the Inquisitor sits with Blackwall and a ledger of names, sorting recruits by what they can do and talking while he whittles. No drills are needed.
