## Premise

The seal on the convoy's waybill belongs to Knight-Captain Ilse Varn, who served beside Cullen in the old Order. He asks to speak with the Inquisitor privately and then takes a long time to say her name.

## Stakes

Cullen's judgment of Varn will shape how the Inquisition approaches Redcrag. If he cannot separate the officer he remembers from what she has become, the siege may be fought on old loyalty or old grief.

## Beats

1. Cullen's office: maps pushed aside, the seal on the desk between them.
2. He describes Varn as she was: steady, honest, unspectacular, a person other templars went to when they were frightened.
3. He admits what he fears: that she believed she was protecting someone when she took the lyrium.
4. The Inquisitor and Cullen weigh what the Inquisition owes her, a trial, a chance to yield, or a final answer.
5. Cullen asks whether he may write to her himself. It is a request, not a plan.

## Choices

- Let Cullen write to Varn or forbid contact: a letter might split her loyalty or tip the quarry; forbidding it keeps secrecy but strains Cullen.
- Offer Varn terms of surrender or prepare only for assault: terms give her a way out; assault focuses the army.
- Share Cullen's confidence with the war table or keep it private: sharing makes planning honest; privacy protects him.

## Encounters

none.

## Outcomes

The siege of Redcrag now carries a personal edge. Varn may answer a letter or may not; the war table records it either way. War-table project starve-the-quarry is better understood, and Cullen's trust in the Inquisitor deepens.

## Low-energy version

A single conversation at the war table or on the battlements at dusk. No travel and no letters need to be sent unless the Inquisitor wants them to be.
